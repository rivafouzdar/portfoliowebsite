Multi-Product Solutions on AWS Marketplace

AWS Marketplace was built for listing one product at a time. I led design on the seller side of multi-product solutions: the Partner Central tools partners use to package software and services into one solution, take it from draft to a public listing, and sell it through a single offer set.

ROLE Lead Senior UX Designer
TEAM PM, SDM, multiple engineering teams, GTM
TIMELINE Jan – Nov 2025
LAUNCHED AWS re:Invent 2025
01 — The problem

Partners sell solutions. Marketplace only let them list products.

ISVs, system integrators, channel partners, and distributors already sell outcomes offline: their own software, other vendors' products they resell, and the services to tie it all together, pitched as one answer to a problem like zero-trust security or getting data ready for AI.

In Marketplace, that package had to be broken apart into separate listings and separate private offers, with nothing tying them together. Partners lost their story, and buyers were left to rebuild the bundle themselves and approve every purchase on its own. Partners had been asking for a way to sell solutions in Marketplace for years, and this project finally delivered it.

04 — Process

Designed with partners, in the room

Multi-product solutions is a new way for partners to sell, so I wanted the people who would use it involved from the start. I tested designs with ISVs, system integrators, channel partners, and distributors at every stage, from early concepts to near-final flows.

Focus groups
Hours of group sessions
I walked groups of partners through the creation and offer set flows to find where their real-world bundles didn't fit the model.
Partner 1:1s
Deep dives by seller type
One-on-one sessions with individual partners showed how an ISV, an SI, and a distributor each package and price a solution differently.
EMEA · APJ
Partner summits abroad
I traveled to AWS partner conferences and summits in EMEA and APJ to present the designs and get feedback from partners who wanted this feature.
Naming the feature

Research changed the product's name

The feature started as "composable solutions." Partners kept stumbling over the term: it described how the feature was built, not what they were selling. I ran surveys and spoke with partners directly to test terminology across the whole feature, wrote the proposal doc to rename it, and saw it through legal review. It launched as multi-product solutions.

06 — Reflection

Adding a new selling model to a mature platform isn't only a UI problem. Engineering and I had several disagree-and-commit moments along the way. I worked closely with them to define the API model for the solution entity, and we built it out in a customer-obsessed way so the underlying structure matched how buyers and partners actually think about solutions.
