Case study 03 Buyer UX · Conversion

Search & product discovery on AWS Marketplace

Search is the busiest page in AWS Marketplace, but its results were a long, text-heavy list. I redesigned it as a grid of scannable product cards, with actions right on each card, to get more buyers from search to product pages and, ultimately, to increase conversion.

ROLELead Senior UX Designer
TEAMPM, SDM, search engineering, data science
TIMELINEApr – Jun 2026
STATUSLive on AWS Marketplace
01 — The problem

A wall of text between buyers and the product they need

The old results page gave each listing a full-width row: a logo, a title link, and a long, cut-off description. Only three or four results fit on screen, similar listings from the same seller were hard to tell apart, and the details buyers use to decide (pricing model, free trial, delivery method) were hidden until they clicked through.

The only action was the title link. Buyers had to open each product page to see if it was worth a closer look, so many left search without opening any.

03 — Card explorations

Five card explorations, tested with real buyers

Each card had to hold two different kinds of links. Available options are other versions of the same product, like a different pricing tier or bandwidth, and open that version's product page. The call-to-action (CTA) button is the main action for the product on the card, like "Try for free" or "View purchase options". I explored five layouts for fitting both, plus badges, without making the grid uneven or making the two compete for the same click. We then ran A/B tests with users to see which placement was most discoverable and led to the highest conversion.

A
Options listed, right-aligned CTA Listed the first two options inline. Clear, but made cards much taller and uneven in the grid.
B
Side-by-side options + CTA Compact, but "+2 options" and the button sat side by side and competed for the same click.
CShipped
Options link above a full-width CTA Both actions at the bottom of the card: a text link to see options, then one full-width button. Clear hierarchy and consistent card heights.
D
Options above the description Options were easy to spot, but pushed the description down and split the card's actions between top and bottom.
E
Primary badge moved top right Freed up the badge row, but the corner badge was easy to miss and read like an ad.
04 — Process

Tested on the highest-traffic page

Because search gets so much traffic, even small changes to the layout affect a huge number of buyers. I explored several card layouts and grid densities, ran usability sessions with enterprise buyers, and worked with data science on an A/B test. We then rolled the change out gradually over about a month, watching engagement at each step before launching to everyone.

We ran 8 usability sessions with enterprise buyers and a 4-week A/B test against the old list view. Two other variants, a denser four-column grid and a list with larger thumbnails, tested worse on click-through and were cut.
