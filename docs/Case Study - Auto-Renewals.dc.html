Case study 02 Buyer + Seller

Private offer auto-renewals on AWS Marketplace

Most renewals aren't a new decision. The buyer wants to keep the software and the seller wants to keep the customer, but every term still meant rebuilding the offer and chasing approvals. I owned UX across both sides: sellers set renewal terms once, buyers agree once, and each cycle then renews on its own, with either side able to opt out.

ROLE Lead, sole senior UX Designer
TEAM PM, four engineering teams, solutions architects, GTM, UAT team

01 — The problem

Renewing a contract meant starting from scratch every time

A private offer in Marketplace ended on its expiration date. To keep going, the seller built a new offer and the buyer ran it back through procurement to accept it, every term, even when nothing had changed.

The work was in the process, not the decision. For buyers, a late purchase order or an offer that wasn't rebuilt in time could cut off software they depended on. For sellers, the effort of tracking and rebuilding renewals on smaller deals could cost more than the margin, so those renewals lapsed or were never brought into Marketplace.

04 — Process

One feature, two consoles

Whatever a seller sets in their management portal, a buyer has to read and agree to in procurement, and then manage months later in from their subscriptions page. I designed both sides together so that each term, deadline, and price change is named and shown the same way wherever it appears.

05 — Advocating for buyers

Automatic shouldn't mean locked in

Sellers asked for auto-renewals, but buyers are the ones whose money renews without anyone clicking a button. I pushed for protections to ship with the feature at launch rather than later, because a buyer who gets surprised by one renewal won't agree to another.

08 — Reflection

Automation only works when both sides trust it. The hardest part wasn't the renewal logic. It was making every automatic step visible and easy to undo, so buyers felt protected and sellers felt confident offering it. I fought hard to keep flexibility a core tenet for both sides, and even convinced leadership to delay launch so that opting out stayed easy and user trust stayed intact.
