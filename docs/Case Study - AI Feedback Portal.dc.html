Case study 04 Internal tool · AI evaluation

A feedback portal for evaluating AI agents

Our science team needed Marketplace experts to judge AI agent outputs, row by row, at scale. I designed and built the whole thing myself: a production-ready internal app, shipped in about two weeks. I vibe-coded it end to end with Kiro, a couple of weeks after I first got access to Claude and before I had access to Claude Code.

01 — The problem

AI agents need expert judgment, and experts aren't labelers

The science team was running pilot studies on Marketplace AI agents, including agent search quality and partner eligibility predictions. The only people who could judge those outputs were subject-matter experts: partner managers, development managers, and solutions architects.

These evaluators aren't data labelers. From their point of view, the task is simple: here's what our agent said, is it right? They review each response against their own expertise and answer a few questions about it. Admins needed to turn any dataset into a study, assign the right people, and track their progress, all while keeping sensitive data secure.

04 — Reviewer view Reviewer

Reviewers judge one agent response at a time

Reviewers are internal SMEs: partner managers, development managers, solutions architects, and other Marketplace specialists who know what a good answer looks like. Evaluation isn't their main job, so the experience had to be quick to learn and easy to fit between other work.

Reviewers never see the setup. They sign in, see only what's assigned to them, and go through each task: a real user query, the agent's response, and the survey questions the admin built.

06 — Process

A designer shipping code, spec first

This was fully self-led. There was no PM and no requirements doc, just a need from the science team, so I defined the product myself. I used Kiro's spec-driven workflow from start to finish: instead of prompting screen by screen, I wrote three specs before any code: requirements with user stories and acceptance criteria, a design doc covering architecture, data models, and APIs, and an ordered task plan with each task linked to a requirement.

The specs kept the AI on track. Every change could be traced back to a requirement, I could scope work in small commits, and when feedback changed the plan, I updated the spec first and let the code follow.

This wasn't a prototype. I built every part of the app myself: the front end and all its features, the data model and database setup, sign-in and admin permissions, security and data-sensitivity reviews, and integrations with internal services like the employee directory. The first version was live for the team in under a week, and I kept iterating on it based on their feedback about the admin model, data storage, and data sensitivity.

At the very end, I worked with a senior engineer to make sure the app passed every internal security and safety review and was ready for beta.

08 — Design system

Cloudscape where it helps, custom where it doesn't, and fast for reviewers

I used a hybrid approach. Cloudscape components handle the data-heavy views, like task tables, alerts, and loading states, where its built-in accessibility saved a lot of work. The survey builder, live preview, and evaluation screens are custom, but follow Cloudscape's visual language and dark mode.

The audience made the call. Evaluators are business experts, not developers, so the tool needed to feel friendlier and less like a console while still looking at home at Amazon.

Accessibility and speed shaped the evaluator experience. Reviewers can get through a whole study without touching the mouse: single keys answer each question type, arrow keys move between tasks, Tab jumps to notes, and "?" opens a shortcut legend that only shows the keys for the current question. Visible focus rings, skip links, and ARIA labels on custom controls keep it usable with a keyboard or screen reader.
